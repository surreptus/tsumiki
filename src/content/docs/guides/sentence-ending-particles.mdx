:::note[Where the function model stops]
A few sentence endings are real functions: [でしょう](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/deshou/), [だろう](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/darou/) and [かもしれない](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/kamo_shirenai/) take a clause and return a clause, so the Reference covers them. The small particles here (ね, よ, な, さ) and the explanatory んだ add no meaning to the sentence itself. They show how the speaker stands toward the listener, and that depends on the moment, so there is nothing to compose.
:::

## Where they go

Sentence-ending particles (終助詞) come last, after the finished predicate and any politeness ending:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Clause | 行く |
| Polite | 行きます |
| Particle | 行きますよ |

You can often stack them in a fixed order: よ before ね, as in 行きますよね.

## ね and よ

The two most common. Both are about the listener.

| Compare | ね | よ |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker's move | Seeks agreement, shares the feeling | Gives information the listener may not have |
| Like in English | "…, right?" / "…, isn't it?" | "I'm telling you…" / "you know" |
| Example | いい天気ですね。 | もう始まっていますよ。 |
| Means | Nice weather, isn't it? | It has already started. |

Mistakes to avoid:

- **よ can sound pushy.** Used with someone senior, it can feel like correcting them. 来ますよ is fine, but 違いますよ may come across as blunt.
- **ね is not only a question.** With falling pitch it shares an opinion. With rising pitch it checks: 明日ですね？ (It's tomorrow, right?)
- **Both together, よね,** means "I think so and want you to confirm": 難しいですよね。

## か

か at the end turns a sentence into a question. In polite speech it is the question mark: 行きますか。 In casual speech the question is shown by rising pitch alone, and か appears only in blunt or rough speech, so 行く？ is normal and 行くか？ is male and abrupt.

## の and んだ: the explaining ending

This is the hardest ending for learners, because English has no equivalent.

の (or んだ, its spoken form) makes a sentence a **statement of explanation**. The speaker is not simply reporting; they are giving a reason, asking for one, or showing they care about the background.

| Plain | With explaining ending |
|---|---|
| 行かない。I'm not going. | 行かないんだ。I'm not going (and here's the thing). |
| どうしたの？ | What happened? (I can see something's wrong) |
| 遅れました。 | 遅れたんです。 It's that I was late. (an explanation) |

Three common jobs:

| Job | Example | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Give a reason | 頭が痛いんです。 | "The thing is, I have a headache." |
| Ask for a reason | どうして泣いているの？ | "Why are you crying?" |
| Show you noticed | 雨が降っているんだ。 | "Oh, it's raining." |

The form is simple. In casual speech, plain form + んだ or の. In polite speech, plain form + んです.

| Predicate | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb, い-adjective | plain form + んだ | 食べるんだ / 高いんだ |
| な-adjective | stem + なんだ | 静かなんだ |
| Noun | noun + なんだ | 学生なんだ |

Nouns and な-adjectives take な before ん, the same な as in 静かな町. 
When to use it: when you are answering "why", reacting to something you see, or softening a request. When not to: for plain, neutral facts such as a self-introduction. 私は学生なんです sounds like you are explaining something, so use 私は学生です.

Questions with の are soft and common in casual speech, and women, children and friends use them all the time: 行くの？ Questions ending in かい or だい are older and rougher.

## だろう, でしょう and じゃない

These are the guessing and checking endings.

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| だろう / でしょう | I suppose; I expect | 明日は雨だろう。 / 雨でしょう。 |
| だろう? / でしょう? | right? (expects yes) | これ、おいしいでしょう？ |
| じゃない？ | Don't you think? | いいんじゃない？ |
| じゃないですか | You know, don't you? | 昨日言ったじゃないですか。 |

だろう and でしょう are functions; see their pages for how they attach. As an ending, the rising form seeks agreement, like ね with a little more confidence.

じゃない with rising pitch is not a negative. It is a soft way to say "isn't it so?" or to suggest something: そろそろ行こうじゃない？ (Shall we get going?). A falling pitch makes it a real negative: 行かないじゃない。

## な, さ, わ, ぞ, ぜ

These are less common and carry a lot of personality. Use them to understand, not to copy, until you are comfortable.

| Particle | Feel | Who tends to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| な | musing, or a plain command | men, casual | 寒いな。 / 行くな。 |
| さ | a casual "you see" | casual, either gender | それがさ、困ってるんだ。 |
| わ | soft emphasis | traditionally women; also regional | 行くわ。 |
| ぞ | firm statement | rough, male | 行くぞ。 |
| ぜ | cocky statement | rough, male | 行くぜ。 |

な after a dictionary-form verb is a prohibition: 行くな (Don't go). The same sound at the end of an adjective is just musing: 寒いな. See [な (prohibitive)](/reference/suffixes/dictionary/na_prohibitive/) for the prohibition.

## かな, かしら and かも

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| かな | I wonder (to oneself) | 来るかな。 |
| かしら | I wonder (traditionally feminine) | 来るかしら。 |
| かも | maybe (short for かもしれない) | 来るかも。 |

かも is the casual form of [かもしれない](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/kamo_shirenai/).

## Filler and discourse words

These are not at the end of sentences, but they work the same way: they manage the conversation.

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| あの / えっと | A pause while thinking | えっと、明日は… |
| ええ / うん / はい | "Yes", "I'm listening" | はい、分かりました。 |
| まあ | Softening, "well" | まあ、いいか。 |
| ところで | Changing the subject | ところで、明日は? |
| でも / だから | "But", "so" at the start of a sentence | でも、行きたい。 |

Listening sounds (あいづち) like ええ, うん, そうですね, なるほど are expected in Japanese conversation. Staying silent while the other person talks can feel cold.

## Summary

| Want to… | Use |
|---|---|
| Ask for agreement | ね |
| Give information | よ |
| Explain or ask why | んだ / んです / の |
| Guess | だろう / でしょう |
| Suggest gently | じゃない？ |
| Wonder aloud | かな / かしら |
| Say "maybe" casually | かも |
