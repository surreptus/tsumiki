:::note[Where the function model stops]
Some of what you meet here is a function, and the Reference already covers it: ている, てあげる, たがる, そう. The rest is not. Comparison is a sentence pattern with particles. Giving and receiving depend on who is talking to whom. Transitive and intransitive pairs are vocabulary: 開ける and 開く are two separate words, not one verb in two shapes. For these, there is no rule that turns one into the other, so this guide gives tables to learn.
:::

## Comparison (比較)

### A is more than B: より

| Pattern | Example | Means |
|---|---|---|
| A は B より adjective | 猫は犬より小さい。 | Cats are smaller than dogs. |
| B より A のほうが adjective | 犬より猫のほうが小さい。 | Cats are smaller than dogs. (A is the point) |

Both say the same thing. The second puts the focus on A, and is the usual answer to a question.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 犬と猫と、どちらが好きですか。 | 猫のほうが好きです。 |

どちら means "which of the two". It is polite; どっち is casual.

### Equal: と同じ and ほど

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A は B と同じ | A is the same as B | これは私のと同じです。 |
| A は B ほど〜ない | A is not as 〜 as B | 今日は昨日ほど寒くない。 |

ほど is always with a negative. For "as 〜 as", use と同じくらい: 昨日と同じくらい寒い.

### The most: 一番 and の中で

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| 一番 + adjective | 一番高い山 (the tallest mountain) |
| [group] の中で + 一番 | 日本の中で一番高い山は富士山です。 |
| [group] で + 一番 | クラスで一番背が高い。 |

の中で is for groups with three or more items. For two, use ほう. You can drop the group when it is obvious: 何が一番好きですか。

### Getting more or less: ますます and もっと

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| もっと | even more (a wish or request) | もっと食べたい。 |
| ますます | more and more (a trend) | ますます寒くなる。 |
| 少し | a bit | 少し高い。 |
| かなり | quite | かなり高い。 |

## Only, about, and extent

These attach to a noun or number and say how much. They are not a chain of functions; they are small words with a feel of their own. [ばかり](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/bakari_only/) is the one with a Reference page.

| Word | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| だけ | only, neutral | 水だけ飲んだ。 |
| しか〜ない | nothing but, and it is too little | 水しか飲まなかった。 |
| ばかり | nothing but, and it is too much | 肉ばかり食べる。 |
| ほど | about, or to the extent that | 三時間ほど待った。 / 死ぬほど疲れた。 |
| くらい / ぐらい | about | 十分ぐらいかかる。 |
| でも | or something; even | お茶でもどうですか。 |

しか always goes with a negative verb, and だけ is the neutral choice with a positive one. だけ and ばかり replace が and を, but other particles stay: 友達にだけ話した.

## Giving and receiving (授受)

Japanese has three verbs for passing things, and the choice depends on who gives and who receives.

| Verb | Direction | Subject is |
|---|---|---|
| あげる | away from the speaker's side | the giver |
| くれる | toward the speaker's side | the giver |
| もらう | toward the receiver | the receiver |

The key is **in-group and out-group**. You, your family and people close to you are on the speaker's side.

| Giving | Example |
|---|---|
| I give someone | 私は友達に本をあげた。 |
| Someone gives me | 友達は私に本をくれた。 |
| I receive from someone | 私は友達に本をもらった。 |

The last two describe the same event from different sides. くれる has the giver as the subject, and もらう has you.

If someone gives to a third party, あげる is neutral: 母は妹にケーキをあげた。 But if the receiver is closer to you than the giver, くれる is more natural: 母は妹にケーキをくれた (Mum gave my sister the cake).

### Polite forms

| Plain | Polite form | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| あげる | さしあげる | you give to someone above you |
| くれる | くださる | someone above you gives to you |
| もらう | いただく | you receive from someone above you |

先生は私に本をくださった。 私は先生に本をいただいた。 See [Keigo](/guides/keigo/).

### Doing a favour: ~てあげる, ~てくれる, ~てもらう

Add て to the verb for "do something for someone".

| Pattern | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜てあげる | I do it for someone | 友達に教えてあげた。 |
| 〜てくれる | Someone does it for me | 友達が教えてくれた。 |
| 〜てもらう | I get someone to do it | 友達に教えてもらった。 |

See [てあげる](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_ageru/), [てくれる](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_kureru/), [てもらう](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_morau/) and [ていただく](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_itadaku/).

Use てあげる sparingly. It can sound like you are doing a favour from above, so with a superior it is rude. Say 手伝いましょうか instead of 手伝ってあげましょうか.

## Transitive and intransitive pairs (自動詞・他動詞)

A **transitive** verb takes an object (を). An **intransitive** one does not. Many pairs come as two different words:

| Meaning | Transitive (を) | Intransitive |
|---|---|---|
| open | 開ける | 開く |
| close | 閉める | 閉まる |
| turn on | つける | つく |
| turn off | 消す | 消える |
| start | 始める | 始まる |
| stop | 止める | 止まる |
| break | 壊す | 壊れる |
| drop | 落とす | 落ちる |
| put in | 入れる | 入る |
| take out | 出す | 出る |
| change | 変える | 変わる |
| line up | 並べる | 並ぶ |
| gather | 集める | 集まる |
| raise | 上げる | 上がる |
| lower | 下げる | 下がる |
| hide | 隠す | 隠れる |
| fix, heal | 直す | 直る |
| wake | 起こす | 起きる |

### Which is which

The pairs are not fully regular, but patterns help.

| If the transitive ends in… | The intransitive tends to end in… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ~す | ~れる, ~る | 壊す / 壊れる |
| ~eる | ~aる | 閉める / 閉まる |
| ~eる | ~く | 開ける / 開く |
| ~す | ~eる | 消す / 消える |

Those are tendencies and not laws, so learn each pair together.

### How to use them

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Someone did it (transitive) | 私がドアを開けた。 |
| It happened by itself, or you don't say who (intransitive) | ドアが開いた。 |
| A result you can see (intransitive + ている) | ドアが開いている。 |
| A deliberate action with a result (transitive + てある) | ドアが開けてある。 |

The last two are a common test. 開いている is a plain state, and 開けてある means somebody opened it on purpose. See [ている](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_iru/) and [てある](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_aru/).

## Summary

| Task | Use |
|---|---|
| A is more than B | A は B より… / B より A のほうが… |
| The most | 一番 / [group] の中で一番 |
| I give | あげる |
| I receive | くれる (giver subject) / もらう (receiver subject) |
| A favour | 〜てあげる / くれる / もらう |
| By itself | Intransitive |
| By someone | Transitive |
