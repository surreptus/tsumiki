:::note[Where the function model stops]
Each conditional is a function, and the Reference shows how to build it: [と](/reference/suffixes/dictionary/to_conditional/), [ば](/reference/suffixes/kateikei/ba/), [たら](/reference/suffixes/ta_stem/tara/) and [なら](/reference/suffixes/plain-clause/nara/). Building one is easy. Choosing between them is the hard part, because they differ in *what kind of “if”* you mean, and that depends on the situation. This guide is for choosing.
:::

## Forming them

| Conditional | Verb | い-adjective | な-adjective / noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| と | 食べると | 高いと | 静かだと / 学生だと |
| ば | 食べれば | 高ければ | 静かなら(ば) / 学生なら(ば) |
| たら | 食べたら | 高かったら | 静かだったら / 学生だったら |
| なら | 食べるなら | 高いなら | 静かなら / 学生なら |

The negative follows the usual route first: 食べなければ, 食べなかったら, 食べないと, 食べないなら.

## The feel of each

| Conditional | Feel | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| と | Automatic result | "Whenever A, B happens." A leads to B by nature or habit. |
| ば | General condition | "If A holds, B follows." A is the key condition. |
| たら | Once A has happened | "When or if A happens, then B." The safe all-rounder. |
| なら | Given that | "If that is the case…" A is something already known or just said. |

The same idea, spring arriving, said three ways:

| Sentence | What it says |
|---|---|
| 春になると、暖かくなる。 | A natural law: when spring comes, it gets warm. |
| 春になれば、暖かくなる。 | The condition for warmth: if spring comes, it will be warm. |
| 春になったら、花見に行こう。 | After spring arrives, let's go to see blossoms. |

なら is missing because it needs a condition that is already known from the conversation, and “spring arrives” is not that.

## と: automatic results

と is for results that follow without anyone deciding. Natural laws, habits, directions, and discoveries.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Natural law | 水は零度になると凍る。 |
| Habit | 家に帰ると、まずお茶を飲む。 |
| Directions | 右に曲がると、駅があります。 |
| Discovery | ドアを開けると、猫がいた。 |

The result cannot be a request, a command, a wish or your own decision. This is the main limit:

| Wrong | Why | Right |
|---|---|---|
| 駅に着くと、電話してください。 | A request cannot follow と | 駅に着いたら、電話してください。 |
| 安いと、買いたい。 | A wish cannot follow と | 安ければ、買いたい。 |

## ば: the condition itself

ば names the condition on which something depends, so it fits general truths, advice, and states.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Condition for a result | 読めば分かる。 |
| A state as the condition | 安ければ買います。 |
| Advice with いい | 駅で聞けばいいですよ。 |
| Wishing it were so | もっと時間があれば、行けるのに。 |
| The more… the more | 読めば読むほど面白い。 |

With an action as the condition, a result that is your own decision sounds off. 駅に着けば、電話します is unnatural; use たら: 駅に着いたら、電話します. States are fine: 時間があれば、行きます.

## たら: when or if it happens

たら is the most flexible, and the one to use when you are unsure. It means "once A has happened" or "if A happens".

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Plans after something | 家に着いたら、電話する。 |
| A request or suggestion | 医者に行ったらどうですか。 |
| A condition | 雨だったら、行かない。 |
| Discovery (past) | 開けたら、猫がいた。 |

Almost every other conditional can be replaced with たら. It cannot replace なら when the speaker is reacting to what someone has just said, and it cannot replace と when the result is a fixed law (水は零度になったら凍る sounds less like a law and more like a single event).

## なら: given that

なら takes something already known, from context or from the other person, and gives advice or a comment on it. A common pattern is a reply to what someone just told you.

| Conversation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A: 来週、京都に行きます。 | |
| B: 京都に行くなら、清水寺がおすすめです。 | If you are going to Kyoto, I recommend Kiyomizu. |

Two features set it apart:

- **The condition can come after the result in time.** 買い物に行くなら、牛乳を買ってきて。 (If you are going shopping, buy milk.) You decide before you go.
- **It works for nouns and states easily.** 学生なら、割引があります。 (If you are a student, there is a discount.)

You can use なら in the plain, past or negative: 行ったなら, 行かないなら. The condition is about the situation, so it does not have to be a future event.

## Which one

| Ask yourself | Use |
|---|---|
| Does B just happen when A happens, with no choice? | と |
| Am I giving a condition or a general rule? | ば |
| Is it "after A is done", or am I unsure? | たら |
| Am I reacting to what someone said, or to a known fact? | なら |

When in doubt, たら.

## Common fixed patterns

| Pattern | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜ばいい | you should just | 聞けばいい。 |
| 〜といい | it would be good to | 早く寝るといいですよ。 |
| 〜たらどうですか | why not | 休んだらどうですか。 |
| 〜ないと / なければ | if I don't, which implies "I must" | もう帰らないと。 |
| 〜たら〜た | when I did, I found | 開けたら、空だった。 |

The contracted forms are in [Casual speech](/guides/casual-speech/): なければ becomes なきゃ, and ば can become りゃ.
