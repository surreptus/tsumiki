:::note[Where the function model stops]
The *shape* of keigo is mostly functions: お〜になる, お〜する, れる・られる and ます all take a verb and return a verb, so the Reference covers them. Deciding *which* to use is not a function. It depends on who is speaking, who is listening, and who the sentence is about, and none of that is in the word. This guide gives the few rules first, then the judgment.
:::

## Three kinds of keigo (敬語)

| Kind | Japanese | What it does | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polite | 丁寧語 | Makes your whole sentence courteous to the listener | ですます |
| Honorific | 尊敬語 | Raises the person *doing* the action | 召し上がる |
| Humble | 謙譲語 | Lowers *you* (or your side) when your action involves someone else | いただく |

The first is about the listener. The second and third are about a person in the sentence. A single sentence can use all three.

## Who is above, who is below

Keigo follows relationships, not individual worth.

| Raise (honorific) | Lower (humble) |
|---|---|
| Customers, clients | You |
| Teachers, bosses | Your family, to outsiders |
| People you have just met | Your company, to people outside it |
| Anyone older, in many settings | Your own boss, when speaking to a customer |

The last row is the one learners find surprising. To a customer, your own boss is on *your* side, so you use humble forms for them: 部長はただいま出かけております (The department head is out). You do not call them 部長様 or use honorifics. This is called **uchi-soto**, inside and outside.

## Polite: ます and です

You already know this one. Use it by default with anyone you do not know well. See [ます](/reference/suffixes/renyokei/masu/).

## Honorific: raising the other person

Three patterns, from most to least common in formal speech.

| Pattern | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Special verb | A different verb entirely | 食べる → 召し上がる |
| お〜になる | お + ます-stem + になる | 書く → お書きになる |
| れる・られる | The passive form, used as honorific | 書く → 書かれる |

The special verbs come first because they are the most formal, and the most common in daily service speech:

| Plain | Honorific |
|---|---|
| 食べる, 飲む | 召し上がる |
| 見る | ご覧になる |
| 行く, 来る, いる | いらっしゃる |
| する | なさる |
| 言う | おっしゃる |
| くれる | くださる |

If a special verb exists, use it. Otherwise use お〜になる:

<Derivation verb="書く" chain={["o_ni_naru"]} stems />

See [お〜になる](/reference/suffixes/renyokei/o_ni_naru/). Here is how the three patterns compare for one verb:

| Verb | Special | お〜になる | れる・られる |
|---|---|---|---|
| 書く | none | お書きになる | 書かれる |
| 読む | none | お読みになる | 読まれる |
| 食べる | 召し上がる | お食べになる (unusual) | 食べられる |

The last column is easy to build but is the least formal; it looks identical to the passive and the potential, so context decides. It is common in writing and with people who are respected but not far above you.

Requests are honorific too. The standard is お〜ください:

| Plain request | Honorific request |
|---|---|
| 書いてください | お書きください |
| 座ってください | おかけください |
| 待ってください | お待ちください |

Four honorific verbs (いらっしゃる, なさる, くださる, おっしゃる) have an odd ます-form. Their stem ends in い, not り: いらっしゃいます, not いらっしゃります. See [Irregulars](/reference/irregulars/).

## Humble: lowering yourself

Humble forms are for *your own* actions that touch the other person: you give, you receive, you visit, you ask.

| Pattern | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Special verb | A different verb | 言う → 申す |
| お〜する | お + ます-stem + する | 持つ → お持ちする |
| 〜ていただく | Receive the favour of | 教えていただく |

| Plain | Humble |
|---|---|
| 食べる, 飲む | いただく |
| 行く, 来る | 参る |
| いる | おる |
| する | いたす |
| 言う | 申す |
| 見る | 拝見する |
| 会う | お目にかかる |
| 聞く, 訪ねる | 伺う |

<Derivation verb="持つ" chain={["o_suru"]} stems />

See [お〜する](/reference/suffixes/renyokei/o_suru/) and [ていただく](/reference/suffixes/te_stem/te_itadaku/).

Humble forms apply to *you*. Never use them for someone else's action: 先生が申しました is wrong; 先生がおっしゃいました is right.

## Do not mix them up

The most common error is applying the right pattern to the wrong person.

| Wrong | Why | Right |
|---|---|---|
| お客様が参りました | 参る is humble, but the customer is above you | お客様がいらっしゃいました |
| 私が召し上がります | 召し上がる is honorific, but it is your own action | 私がいただきます |
| 部長はいらっしゃいません (to a customer) | Your boss is inside your group, so raising them to an outsider is wrong | 部長はおりません |

To a colleague in the same company, the first version is right. This is uchi-soto again.

## Double honorifics

Piling two honorifics on one verb is considered wrong, even though it is common:

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| お召し上がりになられる | 召し上がる |
| おっしゃられる | おっしゃる |

Each special verb is already honorific, so れる・られる adds nothing but noise.

## Common fixed phrases

Many customer-service phrases are single units you can learn whole.

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Welcome | いらっしゃいませ |
| Thank you | ありがとうございます |
| Please wait | 少々お待ちください |
| Sorry | 申し訳ございません |
| Understood | かしこまりました |
| Thanks for your work (leaving) | お疲れ様でした |
| Excuse me (entering) | 失礼いたします |

ございます is the polite form of ある, and いたします is the humble する. Both are also special verbs.

## Prefixes: お and ご

お and ご make nouns polite or honorific.

| Prefix | Used with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| お | Native Japanese words | お名前, お茶, お金 |
| ご | Sino-Japanese words (kanji compounds) | ご家族, ご連絡, ご説明 |

Both are also humble when the thing is yours and the other person receives it: ご連絡します (I will contact you), お手紙を書きました (I wrote you a letter). There are exceptions, such as お電話 and お元気, so treat the rule as a default.

## Summary

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Any polite conversation | ます, です |
| The other person's action | Special verb, or お〜になる |
| Your own action involving them | Special verb, or お〜する |
| Asking them to do something | お〜ください |
| Talking about your side to outsiders | Humble forms |
