:::note[Where the function model stops]
Suffixes change a word's *shape*, so they can be written as functions. Particles never change a word. They sit after it and say what job it does in the sentence, and the right choice depends on what you mean. There is no input and output to compose, so this guide is a set of contrasts instead.
:::

## What a particle (助詞) does

A particle follows a noun (or a whole clause) and labels its role. Word order is flexible in Japanese because the labels, not the positions, carry the grammar:

| Sentence | Reads as |
|---|---|
| 猫が魚を食べた。 | The cat ate the fish. |
| 魚を猫が食べた。 | The cat ate the fish. (same roles, different emphasis) |
| 猫を魚が食べた。 | The fish ate the cat. |

Swap the particles and you swap the roles. Swap the order and nothing changes.

## The core particles

| Particle | Marks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| が | the subject, or the thing wanted, liked or possible | 水が飲みたい。 |
| を | the direct object, or the place passed through | パンを食べる。 / 道を歩く。 |
| に | a target: where to, when, who to, the result of a change | 駅に着く。 / 三時に会う。 |
| で | where an action happens, or the means | 家で食べる。 / 箸で食べる。 |
| へ | a direction | 学校へ行く。 |
| の | belonging, or "of" | 私の本 |
| と | together with, or a quote | 友達と行く。 / 行くと言った。 |
| も | "also", replacing は, が or を | 私も行く。 |
| から / まで | start and end | 九時から五時まで |
| より | comparison, "than" | 昨日より暑い。 |
| や | an incomplete list, "and such" | りんごやみかん |
| か | a question, or "or" | 行きますか。 |

The rest of this guide covers the pairs that cause trouble.

## は and が

Both can follow the subject, and both are correct in many sentences. They answer different questions.

| Compare | は | が |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Sets the **topic**: what we are talking about | Marks the **subject**: who or what does it |
| New or known | The thing is already known | The thing is new, or is the answer |
| Contrast | Often implies "as for this one, unlike others" | Picks one out of the group |
| Example | 私は学生です。 | 私が学生です。 |
| Means | As for me, I am a student. | I am the one who is a student. |

A test: if the sentence answers *who*, *which* or *what*, use が. If it comments on something already on the table, use は.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 誰が来ましたか。 Who came? | 田中さんが来ました。 (new information) |
| 田中さんはどうしましたか。 What about Tanaka? | 田中さんは来ませんでした。 (comment on a known topic) |

Two habits help:

- **Bring something new into the conversation with が**, then refer back to it with は. 昔々、おじいさんがいました。おじいさんは山へ行きました。
- **Inside a small clause, use が.** は rarely appears in a clause that modifies a noun: 私が作ったケーキ, not 私は作ったケーキ.

は can also attach to other particles to add contrast: 家では (at home, at least), 私とは (with me, though). It stacks on に, で, から and the like, but replaces が and を.

## を and が with wanting, liking and ability

Some predicates mark the thing they want, like or can do with が. Others accept either.

| Pattern | Typical | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Liking, hating | が | 犬が好きだ。 |
| Wanting (たい) | が or を | 水が飲みたい。/ 水を飲みたい。 |
| Ability (potential) | が or を | 日本語が話せる。 |
| Knowing, needing | が | 答えが分かる。/ 時間が要る。 |

For 〜たい and the potential form, both work, and が is a little more natural when the object is the focus. See [たい](/reference/suffixes/renyokei/tai/) and [potential](/reference/suffixes/mizenkei/potential/).

## に and で

Both can translate as "at" or "in", and the choice follows the verb, not the place.

| Compare | に | で |
|---|---|---|
| With a verb of **existing** | The place where something is or lives: いる, ある, 住む | |
| With a verb of **doing** | | The place where the action happens |
| Example | 家にいる。 | 家で勉強する。 |
| Means | I am at home. | I study at home. |

If the verb is about being somewhere, use に. If it is an action that takes place there, use で.

Other uses of each:

| に | で |
|---|---|
| A destination: 学校に行く | A means or tool: 電車で行く |
| A point in time: 七時に起きる | A total or limit: 三つで千円 |
| A person receiving: 友達にあげる | A cause: 病気で休む |
| The result of a change: 先生になる | A material: 木で作る |
| The purpose of going: 買い物に行く | |

に with time is only for specific points: 七時に, 月曜日に, 三月に. Words like 今日, 昨日 and 毎日 take no particle.

## へ and に for direction

For "go to", 学校へ行く and 学校に行く mean the same. へ stresses the direction, and に stresses the arrival point. Learners can use に everywhere and be right; へ is optional.

## を, に and で with movement

| Particle | What the place is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| に | the destination | 駅に行く |
| を | the space you pass through or leave | 道を歩く / 家を出る |
| で | the stage where the action occurs | 公園で走る |

## と, や and も

| Particle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| と | A and B, complete list | パンと牛乳 |
| や | A and B among others | パンや牛乳 |
| と | with someone (they join in) | 母と行く |
| に | to someone (they receive) | 母に話す |
| も | also, too | 私も行く。 / パンも牛乳も買った。 |

と for "with" works only when the other person takes part. If they are merely the target, use に: 先生に聞く (ask the teacher), 先生と話す (talk with the teacher).

## の

の joins two nouns: 私の本, 日本語の先生. It can also stand in for a noun that is already known, so you do not repeat it: これは私のです (This is mine). It turns a clause into a noun too, which is covered in [Relative clauses, nominalization, quotation](/guides/clauses/).

## Stacking and dropping

- **Stacking.** Particles can chain: 日本からの友達 (a friend from Japan), 駅までの道 (the road to the station).
- **Dropping.** In speech が, を and は are often left out when the sentence is clear: 水、飲む？ 映画、見た？ Writing and polite speech keep them.

## Summary

| Choose | When |
|---|---|
| は | Talking *about* something known |
| が | Naming *who or what* is new, or the answer |
| に | A target, a point in time, a state of being somewhere |
| で | A place of action, or a tool |
| を | The object, or the path you pass through |
| と | Together with, or a complete list |
| や | A partial list |
