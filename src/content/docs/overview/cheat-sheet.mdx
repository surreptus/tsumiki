## 1. Stems: the shape a verb takes

Ichidan verbs (食べる, 見る) drop る and add the ending. Irregular verbs (する, 来る) have their own stems on the [Irregulars](/reference/irregulars/) page.

## 2. Order: inside to outside

<LayerTable />

> **C**ats **P**ounce **A**t **D**ancing **N**oisy **T**in **P**ots

You can skip layers but not go back. The past tense ends the word, so it goes last.

**The one exception is ます.** For verbs it is applied early, before the negative and the past: 食べます, 食べません, 食べました, 食べませんでした. Politeness is still the last thing you hear, but in the notation it comes first, and the past is still on the outside.

## 3. Types: what each step gives

| After… | The word is a… | What can follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| させる, られる, ている | verb | any verb ending |
| たい, ない, やすい | い-adjective | くない, かった, くて, ば |
| た, past forms | a finished word | nothing that needs a stem |
| ます | polite verb | ません, ました, ましょう |
| だ, です | a statement | か, ね, よ, けど |

## 4. Three checks when a form feels wrong

1. **Right stem?** Look up the ending: it names the one stem it takes.
2. **Right order?** Causative, passive, aspect, want, not, past, polite. With ます, put it first: 食べません, then 食べませんでした.
3. **Right type?** An い-adjective cannot take たい, and a verb cannot take です.

The [common mistakes](/overview/common-mistakes/) page shows the usual slips, and the [conjugator](/reference/conjugator/) checks a chain step by step.
