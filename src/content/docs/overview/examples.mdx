## A passive with negative and past

書かれなかった means "was not written (by someone)":

<Derivation verb="書く" chain={["passive", "neg", "past"]} stems />

The passive attaches to 書か, the ない-stem, as れる. That gives a verb. The negative then makes an い-adjective, so the past is なかった.

## An ongoing state, denied, in the past

行っていなかった means "was not going / had not gone":

<Derivation verb="行く" chain={["te_iru", "neg", "past"]} stems />

Notice the first step uses 行って, not 行いて. 行く has an irregular て-form, and the model records that exception in one place so every form built on it is correct.

## Polite, passive, negative, past

来られませんでした means "could not come" (or "was affected by someone coming"):

<Derivation verb="来る" chain={["passive", "masu", "neg", "past"]} stems />

Here ます comes before the negative and the past. The ます-form has its own small set of endings (ません, ました, ませんでした), so the negative becomes ません and the past becomes でした.

## An adjective with です

食べたかったです means "wanted to eat" (politely):

<Derivation verb="食べる" chain={["tai", "past", "desu"]} stems />

For い-adjectives, です only adds politeness, so the tense goes **inside** and です goes last. 食べたいでした would be a type error.

## A noun that behaves like an adjective

静かじゃなかった means "was not quiet":

<Derivation verb="静か" chain={["neg", "past"]} stems />

じゃない turns 静か into an い-adjective, and an い-adjective forms its past with かった.

## Build your own

The [conjugator](/reference/conjugator/) lets you pick any word and click suffixes in order. When a chain is invalid, you get the same kind of type error as above. The [Reference](/reference/stems/renyokei/) lists every stem and suffix with notes on when to use each.
