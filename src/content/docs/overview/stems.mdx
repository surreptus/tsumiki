A **stem** is the form of a verb that is ready to take an ending. It is a function too: `renyokei(書く)` gives 書き, and `mizenkei(書く)` gives 書か. The verb is the same, but the shape that comes out depends on which stem you ask for.

<StemsArt />

A stem is always the first half of a pair: it fits the word, then a suffix attaches. `tai(renyokei(書く))` is `renyokei` making 書き, then `tai` making 書きたい. The [idea page](/overview/idea/) has the full picture.

## A verb has a handful of stems

There are only seven for verbs. The table shows each one for 書く, with what it is used for:

| Stem | Plain name | 書く gives | Used before |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 連用形 | the ます-stem | 書き | ます, たい, ながら |
| 未然形 | the ない-stem | 書か | ない, れる, せる |
| 意志形 | the "let's" stem | 書こ | う |
| 仮定形 | the ば-stem | 書け | ば |
| 命令形 | the command form | 書け | (used alone) |
| て形 | the て-form | 書いて | いる, しまう, おく |
| た形 | the た-form | 書いた | ら, ことがある |

## Godan verbs move along the kana chart

書く ends in く. To get the other stems you shift that last sound along its row of the kana chart:

| Row | く becomes | Gives | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| い-row | き | 書き | ます, たい |
| あ-row | か | 書か | ない, れる, せる |
| お-row | こ | 書こ | う |
| え-row | け | 書け | ば, commands, "can" |

Every godan verb does the same with its own last sound. 飲む becomes 飲み, 飲ま, 飲も, 飲め. 話す becomes 話し, 話さ, 話そ, 話せ.

The て-form and た-form are the exception. Their ending changes by sound, such as 書いて, 飲んで, 待って and 話して. You can see every case on the [て-form stem page](/reference/stems/te_stem/).

## Ichidan verbs collapse into one stem

Verbs like 食べる are simpler. You drop る and several stems turn out identical:

<StemTable verb="食べる" />

The ます-stem, the ない-stem and the "let's" stem are all 食べ, and the て-form and た-form are just 食べ plus て or た. That is why ichidan verbs feel easy. There is almost nothing to learn once you have 食べ.

## What the names mean

Each stem has a traditional name made of kanji, and the characters are a handy memory hook. Most end in 形 (*kei*, "form"), so you only need to remember the first part. Each stem's own page breaks the characters down one by one.

## Two verbs break the pattern

する and 来る have their own stem rules. 来る even changes its pronunciation: 来ない is こない, but 来ます is きます. The [irregulars](/reference/irregulars/) page lists every exception, including honorific verbs such as いらっしゃる.
