Tsumiki is not a course. It has no exercises, audio, vocabulary lists or dialogues, and it does not teach kanji. A textbook or guide such as Genki, Marugoto or Tae Kim's guide does that far better. Keep using one, and open Tsumiki when you want to see how the endings fit together.

## What it adds

| When you… | Tsumiki gives you |
| --- | --- |
| meet a long form like 食べさせられたくなかった | the pieces in order, with the result of each step |
| wonder why a form is wrong | the reason: which ending needs which kind of word |
| see an ending you do not know | a lookup by what it attaches to, with examples |
| learn the same ending in three lessons | one page that collects it |
| mix up two endings | a side-by-side comparison on each page |
| open a dictionary or a Japanese grammar book | the traditional names, such as 連用形 and 未然形, next to plain English |

## A way to use both

1. Learn a form from your course, with its examples and exercises.
2. Open its page here and see which stem it takes and what it gives back.
3. When a longer form appears in your reading, take it apart in the [conjugator](/reference/conjugator/).

If your course and a page here seem to disagree, trust your teacher and check the page's notes. The aim is to give the same facts in another order, not different ones.
