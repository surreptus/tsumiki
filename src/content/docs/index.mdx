Long Japanese forms look like one hard word to memorise. They are not. 食べさせられたくなかった ("did not want to be made to eat") is five small pieces, each with one job, clicked together like building blocks (積み木, *tsumiki*).

:::caution[Not yet reviewed]
The Japanese on this site has not yet been checked by a native speaker, so some examples and notes may be wrong or unnatural. If you spot one, please [report it](https://github.com/surreptus/tsumiki/issues/new?template=wrong-japanese.yml).
:::

| Step | Add | Meaning added | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | | the verb | 食べる, eat |
| 1 | させ | make someone do it | 食べさせる, make eat |
| 2 | られ | it happens to you | 食べさせられる, be made to eat |
| 3 | たい | want | 食べさせられたい, want to be made to eat |
| 4 | ない | not | 食べさせられたくない, do not want to be made to eat |
| 5 | た | in the past | 食べさせられたくなかった, did not want to be made to eat |

Every piece works the same way everywhere. Learn it once and you can read, and build, any longer form. The guide writes each step as a small function, so the whole word reads from the inside out:

No programming is needed. Every function comes with a plain table, and the idea is only something goes in and something comes out. The [worked examples](/overview/examples/) trace longer forms step by step.

## What you get

- **Build forms yourself.** Instead of memorising a table per verb, you learn a few stem shapes and a set of small endings, then combine them.
- **See why a form is wrong.** 食べないたい fails for a reason you can read: ない gives an adjective, and たい only attaches to a verb. The [common mistakes](/overview/common-mistakes/) page collects the usual ones.
- **Look up any ending.** Each page shows how the ending attaches, examples, and how it differs from the endings learners confuse it with.
- **Check your own forms.** The conjugator builds a chain step by step and explains any error.

- **Want to try it?** Open the [conjugator](/reference/conjugator/), or print the [cheat sheet](/overview/cheat-sheet/).
- **Past the endings?** Not everything combines. [The Guides](/guides/particles/) cover particles, keigo, conditionals and casual speech, and each one starts by saying where the building-block view stops.
